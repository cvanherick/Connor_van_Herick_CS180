Project 1

Images of the Russian Empire

Reconstructing color photographs from Prokudin-Gorskii glass plate scans by aligning the red and green exposures to the blue channel.

CS180 Project 1 assignment · 14 provided images and 3 selected examples

Approach

From Three Plates to One Color Image

Each source image stores the blue, green, and red exposures stacked vertically. I split the image into thirds, aligned the green and red channels to blue, and report every displacement as (dy, dx): positive dy moves a channel downward and positive dx moves it right.

I implemented three alignment pipelines: Single-Scale Raw NCC for the small JPEGs, Multi-Scale Raw NCC for the required pyramid baseline, and Final Edge-NCC Pyramid for the submitted results. The final version keeps the same pyramid search but scores gradient-magnitude images instead of raw intensities.

The raw-NCC baseline scores the whole shifted channel, including borders. The final method instead scores gradient magnitudes on the center crop to reduce border interference. After alignment, I stack the channels in RGB order, crop the outer frame, and apply a contrast stretch. These last two operations are postprocessing.

1 Single-Scale Raw NCC splits B, G, and R, then exhaustively searches shifts for small JPEGs.
2 Multi-Scale Raw NCC adds a coarse-to-fine pyramid for large TIFF files.
3 Final Edge-NCC Pyramid uses the same pyramid, but scores gradient magnitudes.

NCC Formula

I score candidates with sum((a-mean(a))*(b-mean(b))) / (||a-mean(a)||*||b-mean(b)||).

Here a is the candidate shifted channel and b is blue. Mean subtraction removes a constant brightness offset; normalization removes an overall scale difference. I maximize this score over integer translations, independently for green and red.

Pyramid Alignment

The pyramid downsamples by 0.5 with anti-aliasing until the smaller side is at most 300px, searches radius 15 at the base, doubles the displacement at each finer level, then refines within radius 5.

Crop and Postprocessing

Final edge scoring uses the middle 50% of each dimension; raw scoring uses the full channel. After stacking, the final pipeline crops 7% off each side and stretches intensities from the global 1st to 99th percentile.

Runtime

A previous individual Prisoners run took about 23 seconds. This is an observed run time, not a per-image benchmark for the full collection. Both pipelines use a fixed parameter set, with no image-specific tuning.

Required Baseline

Multi-Scale Raw NCC

I first implemented the required coarse-to-fine pyramid using raw-pixel NCC. Several channels failed because of border interference and differences in brightness structure. I retained the same pyramid and NCC search, replaced raw intensities with gradient magnitudes, and restricted scoring to the image interior.

The three JPEGs reach the base case directly, giving Single-Scale Raw NCC: two nested loops test every integer shift in [-15, 15] along each axis (961 candidates per channel). Larger inputs use Multi-Scale Raw NCC. I kept the raw-NCC outputs for comparison during development, but the main results below show the cleaner final pipeline: each grayscale source plate next to its final color reconstruction. Final Results for High-Resolution TIFFs

For the larger TIFF files, exhaustive search at full resolution would be slow because the true displacements can be dozens or hundreds of pixels. The pyramid first solves a smaller version of the problem, doubles that displacement at each finer level, and refines locally. Bells & Whistles

Edges, Cropping, and Contrast

Raw RGB intensities are not always comparable across channels. The Emir portrait is the clearest example: the blue channel's brightness pattern differs from red and green, so raw pixel NCC can choose a displacement that looks numerically good but visually wrong. Edge-based NCC helps because it compares structural changes rather than absolute brightness.

For better features, I approximate horizontal and vertical derivatives with forward differences and compute sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy). NCC then compares these edge magnitudes on the image interior. The before-and-after examples combine this feature change with interior scoring and postprocessing, so they do not isolate the effect of edges alone.

Automatic contrast uses the global 1st and 99th intensity percentiles of the stacked image: clip((I - p1) / (p99 - p1), 0, 1). This expands the useful intensity range while clipping extreme values. The fixed 7% border crop is a simple cleanup step, not the assignment's automatic border-detection extension. I did not implement white balance, color remapping, or rotation/scale estimation.

Failure Analysis

Repeated Horizons and Low-Texture Regions

Sunset was the hardest image I tried from the larger collection. Its sky and water contain broad smooth regions, while the channels differ strongly in brightness and color. Because NCC rewards similar intensity structure, both raw pixels and edge scoring can latch onto the wrong repeated horizon or border features instead of the true scene alignment.

The large red displacement and remaining artifacts suggest that the search found an incorrect match. This does not by itself establish that a translation model is insufficient. Stronger border masks or matching sharper interior features could improve the estimate. Sunset is an additional failure study and is not one of the three selected examples.

Notes

Failures and Tradeoffs

The largest failure mode was relying only on raw channel brightness. Some scenes contain strong color-dependent differences, so the NCC score can prefer a displacement that matches intensity patterns but leaves colored ghosts around important edges. Edge-based scoring helps because it compares structure rather than brightness, while final cropping removes the wraparound artifacts introduced by circular shifts.
