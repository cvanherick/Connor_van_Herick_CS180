Project 1

Images of the Russian Empire

Connor van Herick · CS180 Computational Photography

Reconstructing color photographs from Prokudin-Gorskii glass plate scans by aligning the red and green exposures to the blue channel.

Approach

Aligning the Color Channels

Each image starts as one black-and-white glass plate with the blue, green, and red exposures stacked vertically. I split the plate into three equal channels, used the blue channel as the reference, and searched for the green and red shifts that best aligned to it.

For alignment, I used normalized cross-correlation (NCC). The small JPEGs can be aligned with a direct single-scale search. For larger TIFFs, I used an image pyramid: the algorithm first aligns a downsampled version, doubles that displacement at each finer level, and then refines nearby. The pyramid still has the same single-scale search at its base case; it just avoids doing a huge exhaustive search at full resolution.

After alignment, I stacked the channels into an RGB image, cropped the border, and applied a simple contrast stretch. I report displacements as (dy, dx), where positive dy moves a channel downward and positive dx moves it right.

Challenges

Where Alignment Got Hard

The main challenge was that channels do not always have matching brightness patterns. A dark region in one exposure can be bright in another, so raw NCC can choose a shift that has a high score numerically but still leaves visible color ghosts.

Borders were another issue. Because shifting with np.roll wraps pixels around the image, the outer frame can create misleading matches unless the scoring ignores the edges. Sunset was the clearest failure case: the repeated horizon, low-texture sky, and strong color differences made both the raw and improved methods latch onto unreliable structure.

Sunset

The repeated horizontal bands and smooth sky/water regions made this image much less reliable than the successful selected examples.

Improvements

Raw NCC vs. Final Edge-Based Alignment

I improved the basic NCC pipeline by comparing edge magnitudes instead of raw intensities, scoring only the image interior, cropping the final borders, and applying a contrast stretch. The examples below show the raw pyramid output next to the final output.

Emir

Raw intensity matching struggles because the channels have different brightness patterns. Edge matching aligns the face and clothing more cleanly.

Harvesters

The final version reduces colored ghosting by matching structure inside the image instead of letting borders and brightness dominate.

Church
