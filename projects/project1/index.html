Project 1

Images of the Russian Empire

Connor van Herick · CS180 Computational Photography

Reconstructing color photographs from Prokudin-Gorskii glass plate scans by aligning the red and green exposures to the blue channel.

Approach

Aligning the Color Channels

The images start as black-and-white with the blue, green, and red exposures stacked vertically on top of each other. Then, I separated the stack into three separate images, and aligned the green and red with blue as the reference.

For alignment, I used normalized cross-correlation (NCC), which measures similarity after mean-centering and normalizing the two images. To make the alignment more accurate, it only looks at the 50% central area of the image. This prevents borders from distorting the calculation.

NCC(A, B) = Σi(Ai - μA)(Bi - μB) / √[Σi(Ai - μA)2 Σi(Bi - μB)2]

I used a coarse-to-fine image pyramid to efficiently align large images. The algorithm repeatedly downsamples by a factor of two until the smallest dimension is at most 300 pixels. At the base case, it searches displacements within ±15 pixels. At each finer level, it doubles the previous displacement and searches within a five-pixel radius. The direct exhaustive search at the bottom of the pyramid acts as the single-scale base case.

After alignment, I combined the shifted red and green channels with the original blue channel to produce an RGB image. For presentation, I removed a fixed 7% border from each side and applied a contrast stretch that maps the 1st and 99th intensity percentiles to black and white. I report displacements as (dy, dx), where positive dy moves a channel downward and positive dx moves it to the right.

Bells and Whistles

Final Edge-NCC Pyramid

I first implemented the required raw-pixel NCC pyramid. The final version keeps the same search but compares gradient magnitudes, which makes the score rely on image structure instead of brightness differences between exposures. It scores only the central 50% of each image, Gaussian-filters before each 0.5 downsample, crops the wrapped 7% border after alignment, and contrast-stretches the final RGB image. The crop and contrast stretch are postprocessing steps, not alignment steps.

Challenges

Where Alignment Got Hard

The main challenge was that channels do not always have matching brightness patterns. A dark region in one exposure can be bright in another, so raw NCC can choose a shift that has a high score numerically but still leaves visible color ghosts.

Borders were another issue. Because shifting with np.roll wraps pixels around the image, the outer frame can create misleading matches unless the scoring ignores the edges. Sunset was the clearest failure case: the repeated horizon, low-texture sky, and strong color differences made both the raw and improved methods latch onto unreliable structure.
